Stop 01 · Omakase dining

Kushiro Omakase and Japanese Dining

A much-loved spot for great-value Japanese dining

A luxe omakase for less than THB 2,000

The eligible 12-course set starts at THB 1,200 after the discount, before service and tax.

Kik and I ate at Kushiro Omakase twice in the past year, and we chose the 15-course Premium menu both times. Finding omakase of this quality at the same price in central areas in Taiwan or Hong Kong would be a tall order. When we put this guide together, Kushiro had a 4.9 rating and nearly 2,000 reviews on Google Maps. On both visits we saw diners who had come from all over the world.

At the counter, watch the chef make each course

We picked counter seats both times. The chef slices fish and shapes nigiri right in front of you, serving each piece in turn. The meal moves at an easy pace. You can watch the chef work, take a few photos or chat with whoever’s sitting alongside you.

If you’ve never tried omakase, this is a good place to start. The menu follows the seasons, so your meal may look different from ours. That’s part of the fun.

From decorating our glasses to heart-shaped uni and tuna toast

The welcome drink was an occasion in itself. Staff handed us stemmed glasses and a set of colourful markers to draw on them. I wrote the name of our social account on mine. Then came the sweet-tart pink mocktail. A couple of quick doodles made for a fun keepsake photo.

We had tangy Okinawan seaweed, monkfish liver pâté in a tiny cone, truffle chawanmushi and piece after piece of fresh nigiri. The medium-fatty tuna was my favourite. After a few courses of raw fish, chilled truffle udon brought a welcome change in texture and flavour.

What stuck with me most was the crisp, heart-shaped toast topped with sea urchin on one side and minced tuna on the other, served with bubbles and a swirl of dry ice. I also loved the clear fish broth and the eel hand roll. Dessert began with a tamagoyaki that ate like sponge cake and ended with cheesecake.

Four menus to choose from. I’d start with the 15-course Premium

Stop 02 · Five-star hotel

SILQ Hotel & Residence

Our pick for a getaway in central Bangkok

A five-star Bangkok stay popular with international travellers

Do you usually head out early and return to your Bangkok hotel just to sleep? When Kik and I checked into SILQ, our first thought was different: could we clear the schedule and spend all day here?

SILQ is next to Bangkok’s EM District by BTS Phrom Phong, with Emporium, EmQuartier and EmSphere all within easy reach. We booked a two-night staycation and barely ventured farther than the hotel.

We discovered how much fun it can be to do very little. The room was spacious, we took our time at breakfast, and we could book a slot in the private onsen. Even in this central neighbourhood, we spent most of our time at the hotel.

A 56-square-metre room and a surprisingly generous walk-in closet

We stayed in a Chiffon Studio with a balcony. Alongside the double bed were a sofa, dining table, full kitchen, large built-in fridge, microwave and washing machine. The bathroom separated the wet and dry areas and had a roomy bathtub. This felt like somewhere we could happily spend the day.

Kik loved the enormous walk-in closet. The vanity faces a window, letting in the daylight, with wardrobes on both sides. There’s space to dress, do your makeup and get your clothes organised. It’s rare to get a dressing area this roomy in the heart of a city.

I liked that the studio felt like a comfortable apartment. There’s a table to work at, a spot to read, and a sofa or bed to sink into. It’s why we kept wanting to stay in.

Breakfast was so good we skipped lunch twice

The buffet had a lot more choice than we expected. Along with Thai and Western dishes there were Indian, Chinese, Japanese and Korean options, plus eggs and waffles made to order. The waffles, with a choice of jams and syrups, were my favourite.

On both days we ate enough at breakfast to skip lunch. On our second evening we called the front desk and had pad kra pao with pork, a Thai omelette, coconut chicken soup and ice cream delivered to the room. The pad kra pao had a proper kick, and afterwards we could put our feet up without leaving the room or doing the dishes.

Set aside an hour for the private onsen

We booked an hour at the private onsen, alternating between the hot and cold pools and spending time in the sauna. It was one of the highlights of our staycation and a good way to unwind.

The private onsen usually isn’t included in the room rate. Contact the hotel’s THE QLUB on LINE at @theqlub to book. The hotel recommends reserving at least five days ahead to secure a time.

An easy base for shopping in the city centre

The hotel is on Sukhumvit Soi 24. It’s about a 10-minute walk to Emporium, EmQuartier and EmSphere, and BTS Phrom Phong is also within easy walking distance.

If you’d rather avoid the midday heat, the hotel offers a free shuttle to Emporium Tower. It’s scheduled daily from 08:00 to 22:00 and is due to leave from the hotel around the top of each hour. We saw it at the entrance but didn’t ride it. Ask reception to confirm the schedule and where to catch the return shuttle.

Stop 03 · Home-style Thai cooking

กานดาอาหารตามสั่ง

Pad kra pao and tom yum, made to order at street-food prices

The neighbourhood spot for classic pad kra pao

You don’t have to look for a restaurant that specialises in pad kra pao. In Bangkok you’ll see small places called อาหารตามสั่ง (aharn tam sang), which roughly means “cooked to order.” Order pad kra pao, fried rice or stir-fried rice noodles, and the cook starts up the wok.

To friends back in Taiwan, I describe it as the cooking of a stir-fry restaurant served in the single portions you’d get at a noodle shop. The food comes to the table hot from the wok, and you don’t need a whole group to share it. One plate is a meal. It’s an everyday part of eating in Thailand that’s worth trying.

A hidden restaurant, even with a map pin

Kik and I go to กานดาอาหารตามสั่ง beside Central Lat Phrao. It’s on Google Maps, but on the ground you’re looking for a little open-sided stall under a metal awning, with a few tables and no air conditioning. Many people get takeaway or order delivery. Look for the stall and you’ll find it.

We’re there every week, sometimes several times. It’s a simple place, and we go when we want good, affordable everyday Thai food. This is where I’d take someone who wants to see how locals actually eat.

Our usual order: minced pork and a soft fried egg

My favourite is minced pork pad kra pao over rice with a fried egg and a runny yolk. The pork is stir-fried with holy basil, garlic and chilli, with a thick, savoury sauce. Stir the yolk through the rice for an even richer bite.

A plate like this was around THB 65. I like the standard spice level, but it can be quite hot if you’re used to food in Taiwan. Ask up front if you want it mild or without chilli. You can also ask for the egg runny or cooked through.

You can have pad kra pao with seafood, sliced pork, chicken or crispy pork. That’s the nice part of aharn tam sang: pick your protein, spice level and how you’d like your egg.

If there are two of you, share a tom yum

When I go alone, one plate of pad kra pao and an egg is plenty. With two or more people, I’d each order a rice dish and share a bowl of soup.

The prawn tom yum is sour, spicy, fragrant and full of flavour, especially with rice. Our THB 80 bowl came with eight prawns. The stall also serves Thai suki, a dish with plenty of vegetables and glass noodles, if you want to try something different.

Kik likes the fried rice, especially the seafood version with squid and prawns. She also orders pad phrik gaeng, sliced pork stir-fried with red curry paste and spices and served with plain rice. Everything is cooked to order in a hot iron wok and arrives steaming.

Once we ordered pork pad kra pao with an egg, seafood fried rice and tom yum for THB 205 altogether. It’s hard to beat that for a meal for two.

Ordering without speaking Thai

There are so many dishes and variations that you may not see a full checklist-style menu. Locals often write down what they want. You can order aloud or show the friendly staff a photo from the gallery above.

Thai names to show the staff: Minced pork pad kra pao with a runny fried egg: ข้าวกะเพราหมูสับ ไข่ดาวแบบไข่แดงเยิ้ม. Seafood fried rice: ข้าวผัดทะเล. Prawn tom yum: ต้มยำกุ้ง. Garlic chicken rice with a runny fried egg: ข้าวไก่กระเทียม ไข่ดาวไม่สุก. Thai pork suki: สุกี้หมู.

There are few seats, simple furnishings and no air conditioning. It suits anyone who’s happy to eat a quick stir-fry in an open-sided stall. The owner told us they close when they run out of food. I’d aim to get there around 17:00 for dinner so you don’t miss out.

Stop 04 · Affordable massage

Hani Spa

The massage spa we keep coming back to

Boutique-spa atmosphere at neighbourhood prices

A well-regarded spa in central Bangkok can cost thousands of baht. Hani Spa feels like a boutique spa, with a warm space and friendly service, yet plenty of treatments start at just THB 300. I’d make the trip even if you’re staying elsewhere in Bangkok.

Hani Spa has two branches. We go to Branch 2, near Chatuchak Park and next to Union Mall, and that’s the branch in this guide. When we wrote this, both branches had 4.9-star Google Maps ratings and 1,071 reviews between them. We felt comfortable recommending it from our own visits.

Neighbourhood prices, a lovely space

Kik found the spa first when she wanted some relief for a sore neck, shoulders and back. She liked the massage and the prices so much that she brought me along. We’ve been back more than five times.

We like the light, simple wood interior at Branch 2. You can see the welcoming reception area from outside. A traditional Thai massage was THB 300 for an hour or THB 600 for two. A two-hour deep-tissue massage was THB 950.

What our Thai massage was like

We tried a 60-minute traditional Thai massage together. The beds upstairs are separated by curtains. If two of you come together, you can ask to leave the middle curtain open and share the space.

The therapists started by wiping our feet with warm, damp cloths. The pressure was just right and we both nearly drifted off. Kik doesn’t like massages that hurt, and the way the therapists adjusted their pressure is why she keeps going back. Let yours know if you’d like more or less pressure.

Neck and shoulders, deep tissue and a foot massage with a friend

Kik also tried Posture Rescue Massage for the neck and shoulders, which was THB 300 for 60 minutes, and a separate Deep Tissue Massage. The listed deep-tissue prices were THB 550, 750 or 950 for 60, 90 or 120 minutes.

I once brought the Taiwanese creator behind “超強系列” for an hour of foot massage. We sat side by side. He knows Thailand well, visits often and speaks Thai, and he came away full of praise for the place. The menu calls it Reflexology Massage, also THB 300 for an hour.

The little things, from a cold drink to hot tea

After the heat outside, walking into air conditioning and being offered a cold welcome drink feels wonderful. After the massage they brought hot bael tea and little pineapple rolls. The flavour and sweetness reminded me of Taiwanese pineapple cakes.

It’s a simple gesture, but it made me feel looked after from the moment I walked in. I recommend Hani Spa for that as much as for the price. It’s the kind of neighbourhood find where you can set aside an hour and relax in the middle of a trip.

Stop 05 · Lotus pond café

Chowchercof

A lovely spot for afternoon tea in Bangkok

A little hideaway loved by local women

A lotus pond, coconut and pandan desserts, and matcha we keep going back for. If you want a quiet Bangkok café for afternoon tea and photos, this is where I’d take you. Groups of friends often drop by in the afternoon for photos and dessert.

A little café hidden down a side street

Chowchercof is tucked away on Lat Phrao Soi 16. The two-storey white building and natural wood doors and windows are lovely from the moment you arrive. Inside, there’s a broad lotus pond. My favourite spot is a window seat directly facing the water. The open view makes me slow down with the first sip.

The lotuses hadn’t flowered on our last visit, but the still water and surrounding greenery were just as relaxing. It’s near our home, so it’s an easy place to unwind or get some work done. I’d recommend it to friends looking for a cool, comfortable café with character.

The illuminated sign is shaped around the Thai letter ฌ, which appears on the cups too. From the architecture and signage to the seats by the pond, there are plenty of details to notice in the Thai design.

The Coco Ball we order almost every time

Kik and I almost always get the Coco Ball, the coconut and pandan cake I’d most like you to try. It looks like a coconut and has a moist pandan sponge and coconut filling, with just the right sweetness. The coconut and pandan desserts are a big reason we keep returning.

For a drink we’d pick the Matcha Mousse Butterfly Pea. It comes in three beautiful layers and has a fragrant mix of matcha and coconut water. Simpler drinks such as Thai milk tea and Thai black tea were under THB 100. The Thai milk tea frappe is good too, though even with less sugar, I found it as sweet as a full-sugar drink back in Taiwan.

You can also have a proper meal here

There’s more than coffee, matcha and dessert. The menu includes pasta, brunch and Thai dishes, with plenty of mains between THB 100 and 200. It’s more choice than at a typical dessert café, although we haven’t tried the meals yet.

Order by QR code and pay when you leave

Each table has a number and a QR code. Scan it to see the menu in Thai or English, choose what you want and send the order from your phone. Staff will bring it to the table. There’s also a printed menu with photos at the counter.

Pay at the counter before you leave. We usually use Thai QR payment, and the café also takes cash, credit cards and debit cards.

Stop 06 · Vintage shopping mall

Bang Sue Junction

A treasure trove of vintage clothes and antiques

A secondhand shopping paradise with air-con!

If you like secondhand clothes, vinyl or old collectibles and need a break from the heat, I’d go to Bang Sue Junction, known locally as Chatuchak’s Red Building. It’s a six-storey air-conditioned mall opposite Chatuchak Market. Drop by while you’re at the weekend market or come on a weekday to browse at your own pace.

It takes the nickname from the red accents outside. The name may remind people in Taiwan of a red-brick building, but inside it’s a collection of antique, vintage and secondhand shops.

Floors 1 and 2 feel like a free antiques museum

The first and second floors are mainly antique furniture, sculptures and ornaments. Many are large or expensive to take home, but I enjoyed just looking. It feels like a free air-conditioned museum where you can take photos and set your own pace.

If you’re mainly there for clothes, spend a little time on these floors and save the rest for the shops upstairs. Come earlier if you want to take a proper look at the antiques. Some of those shops start closing around 18:00.

Floors 3 to 5 are full of vintage and secondhand clothes

There’s more secondhand fashion upstairs, plus shoes, bags, accessories, old digital cameras and watches. These floors are a good spot to pick up something to take home. Prices vary from shop to shop, so feel free to look around and ask before buying.

I tried on more than five jackets in one shop on the fifth floor. The owner even suggested taking mirror selfies to make it easier to compare them. I picked one GU and one Columbia jacket. The tags were around THB 700 and THB 2,000. The owner knocked THB 350 off, so I paid THB 2,350 for both.

The GU jacket may not have been much cheaper than new, but the Columbia jacket seemed to have retailed for THB 3,000–4,000. It felt like a proper find. The staff were friendly, I felt no pressure while trying things on, and we could talk about the price.

Mono Loop on floor 3, even if you don’t collect records

If you like vinyl, try Mono Loop on the third floor. Artists from Europe, the US and Japan fill the glass displays and the walls. Records range from a few hundred to over a thousand baht, depending on the artist and how hard they are to find.

They also carry secondhand bags, watches and cameras imported from Japan. It’s fun to look at the vintage sleeve designs and take photos with the collectibles even if you don’t buy records.

Fifth-floor egg waffles. Corn in a dessert really works

By the escalator on floor 5, a stand sells popcorn and Hong Kong-style egg waffles. We saw a long line of students and young shoppers. Tick your choices on the order sheet. A plain waffle was THB 39, and one with three toppings was THB 79. Options ran from cheese, Nutella and Oreo to almonds and Thai sweet egg-yolk threads.

Kik suggested fresh banana, corn and chocolate chips, and I loved it. I hadn’t expected corn to work so well in a dessert. The texture’s closer to a regular waffle than the springy Hong Kong version, but the flavour is worth trying.

Next door you’ll find bottled water, cola and other drinks. A bottle of water was THB 10.

A food court and a McDonald’s for a break

There’s a food court on the sixth floor and a McDonald’s on the ground floor. The Thai menu includes local items such as crispy chicken with pad kra pao rice and Thai-style fried dough alongside the familiar burgers, fried chicken and fries.

At the time of writing, McDonald’s and Thai tea brand ChaTraMue had a collaboration with three desserts: a Thai tea Oreo McFlurry, a Thai tea custard pie and fried dough with Thai tea dipping sauce. It was scheduled to run through 5 January 2027. Availability depends on branch stock.

Stop 07 · Bangkok’s “dirty boat”

Saen Saep Express Boat

The Saen Saep canal boat is a shortcut across central Bangkok

Taking the canal when the roads are busy

A cheap express boat runs along a canal through central Bangkok. For some short trips, you can get where you’re going in under 20 minutes without sitting in traffic. We’d heard about it for three years in Bangkok before we finally tried it, and loved it straight away. It also gives you a look at life along the old canal.

Where the “dirty boat” got its reputation

People call the Khlong Saen Saep Express Boat Bangkok’s “dirty boat.” The boats and piers looked old, but we didn’t find them particularly dirty and didn’t notice much smell from the canal. The exhaust from the boats was more noticeable.

I liked seeing people jog, catch up and hang out their washing along the canal. Near the bow you can watch the captain at work. They’re scenes you’d miss from a taxi or the train.

Useful piers to know, from west to east

The boat mostly runs east–west through Bangkok. If it’s your first time, these piers are good starting points for planning a trip:

  • Saphan Hua Chang：West of Pratunam Market and within walking distance of the Siam shopping area, including Siam Paragon, Siam Center, Siam Square One and MBK.
  • Pratunam：Near Pratunam Market and about five minutes on foot from CentralWorld. This is where we boarded.
  • Asoke-Petchaburi：Convenient for MRT Phetchaburi and Makkasan Airport Rail Link. BTS Asok and Terminal 21 are a separate walk or transfer away.
  • Soi Thonglor：A useful point to change transport for the Thonglor and Ekkamai areas.
  • Mahadthai：Farther east. Check the walking route from this pier if you’re going to a concert at Rajamangala National Stadium.
  • The Mall Bangkapi：Our stop that day. Walk through the mall to connect with the MRT Yellow Line.

The list runs from west to east, with other piers in between. Before boarding, use Google Maps to check the direction and your stop. On board you can follow your location and estimate when to get off.

Our rush-hour ride from Pratunam to Bangkapi

We boarded at Pratunam at 16:48 on a Wednesday and reached The Mall Bangkapi at 17:38. That was 50 minutes for a long trip from the centre to the northeastern suburbs. A short hop between central piers takes less time. We would likely have been stuck in traffic if we’d made this trip by car.

As the boat filled, I moved to the front and grabbed a seat. Kik stood all the way. Around 17:00 the boat may save you time while still being crowded. We liked the experience and would use it again when it suits the route.

How to buy a ticket on your first ride

Check the pier map to see whether you’re going east or west. If you’re unsure the boat stops where you want, ask the crew when it pulls in. Hold on or sit down once you board. A fare collector will come by. Tell them your stop, pay the fare and keep the receipt.

We paid in baht cash. Small notes or coins are easiest, and you can wait for the conductor to come to you. Fares depend on distance. The source guide gives THB 16–26 as a reference, with Pratunam to Asoke-Petchaburi at THB 18 and to The Mall Bangkapi at THB 26. Check the current fare at the pier.

Consider the boat on the way to a concert

For Rajamangala National Stadium, one option is to get off at Mahadthai and look up the walk to your specific concert entrance. Traffic can be heavy during big events, so the boat gives you another way in.

Plan the return trip separately. Last departures vary by day, direction and route. The boat may have stopped by the time your concert ends, so check before counting on it for the trip back.

A late-night letter from Chatuchak

When you hear “Chatuchak,” what comes to mind first?

The market that goes quiet on weekdays, the sprawling urban oasis of Chatuchak Park, or the affordable bites and good times at the Castle Night Market?

Two years ago, just after we got engaged, our work as creators was finally gaining traction. We were finding our feet, but we still had to watch every baht.

Moving to Chatuchak was the obvious choice.

Before we knew it, it had become one of our favourite neighbourhoods. Home-style Thai food, good massages at neighbourhood prices, a lotus-pond café, the Castle Night Market and Chatuchak’s Red Building. More golden memories than we can count.

Deep into the night, Asian koels call outside the window. Even at their loudest, they can’t drown out the sound of me typing under a warm lamp, racing to finish this issue.

Thai people made Bangkok what it is, and Chatuchak has given us a place to grow. We put all our energy into making each issue of Thailand Guide PLAY+.
